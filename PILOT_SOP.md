## 1. INTRODUCTION

Welcome to Apex Air! These Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) define how Apex Air pilots operate on the VATSIM network. All pilots are required to read, understand, and comply with these procedures.

**Failure to comply with these procedures may result in disciplinary action up to and including removal from the airline.**

---

## 2. PILOT QUALIFICATIONS & RATINGS

### Required Qualifications
- VATSIM Pilot Account in good standing
- Minimum 50 flight hours on VATSIM
- Minimum S1 Rating
- Passed Apex Air Orientation
- Signed Code of Conduct agreement

### Rating Progression
- **S1-S2:** Limited to VFR operations below 10,000 feet
- **S3:** IFR operations, higher altitudes
- **C1+:** Advanced IFR, complex airports

## 3. PRE-FLIGHT PROCEDURES

### Step 1: Flight Planning (T-1 Hour)
1. Check weather on VATSIM or real-world sources
2. Plan route using Skyvector or similar tool
3. Calculate fuel requirements
4. Determine alternate airports
5. Brief crew on flight plan

### Step 2: Aircraft Preflight (T-30 Minutes)
1. Conduct external walk-around
2. Check fuel quantity and condition
3. Verify aircraft weight and balance
4. Test flight controls
5. Check systems for proper operation

### Step 3: Engine Start & Taxi (T-15 Minutes)
1. Request pushback from Ground
2. Call "pushback approved" (never assume)
3. Request taxi clearance
4. Taxi to runway following ground guidance
5. Perform final checks

### Step 4: Departure
1. Contact Tower
2. Report ready for departure
3. Wait for clearance
4. Perform takeoff on cleared runway
5. Contact Departure once airborne

## 4. IN-FLIGHT PROCEDURES

### Callsign Usage
- **Standard Format:** APX + Flight Number
- **Example:** APX101, APX205
- **Always use proper callsign** on all radio communications

### Altitude Compliance
- Maintain assigned altitude within ±100 feet
- Request altitude changes before initiating
- Follow published SID/STAR procedures
- Comply with airway restrictions

### Speed Requirements
- **Below 10,000 ft:** 250 knots indicated airspeed or less
- **Between 10,000-18,000 ft:** 280 knots or less
- **Above 18,000 ft:** Mach 0.78 or less
- Maintain assigned speed within ±10 knots

### Radio Procedures
```
Good Phraseology:        Poor Phraseology:
"Boston Center,          "Hey Boston!"
AAX101, level flight     "AAX101 up to flight 
level 350"               350, good?"

"AAX101 requesting       "Can we go to 320?"
descent to Flight Level
320"
```

### Position Reports
- Report at designated waypoints
- Include: Callsign, Position, Altitude, Time, Next waypoint
- **Example:** "Boston Center, AAX101 is 50 north of SCITY, flight level 350, estimating BRINY at 1445"

## 5. APPROACH & LANDING

### Descent Planning (T-30 Minutes)
1. Request descent from Center
2. Reduce speed gradually
3. Descend on assigned profile
4. Monitor altimeter and VSI
5. Brief approach procedure

### Approach Briefing
- Review airport layout
- Check runway assignment
- Review approach type (ILS, VOR, etc.)
- Identify go-around procedures
- Set expected minimums

### Landing Procedure
1. Contact Approach 10-15 minutes out
2. Request landing clearance
3. Follow vectors to final approach
4. Configure aircraft for landing
5. Cross threshold at safe height
6. Touch down in first third of runway
7. Maintain centerline during rollout
8. Exit runway on assigned taxiway

### Post-Landing
1. Reduce to taxi speed
2. Contact Ground for parking
3. Follow ramp guidance
4. Proceed to assigned gate
5. Shut down engines

## 6. PROHIBITED MANEUVERS

❌ **DO NOT PERFORM:**
- Barrel rolls
- Loops
- Inverted flight
- Spins
- Aerobatics over populated areas
- Buzzing (low-level flying near airports)
- Excessive speed at low altitude
- Unauthorized deviations from flight plan

**Violations of these rules may result in warnings or removal.**

---

## 7. EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### Engine Failure
1. Declare emergency to ATC
2. Request priority handling
3. Declare PAN/MAYDAY as appropriate
4. Follow published single-engine procedures
5. Plan approach to nearest suitable airport

### Instrument Failure
1. Inform ATC of failed instruments
2. Request vectors to landing
3. Request longest runway available
4. Land as soon as possible

### Loss of Communication
1. Set squawk to 7700
2. Navigate to nearest airport
3. Maintain safe altitude
4. Land when possible

**Always prioritize safety and declare emergencies when appropriate.**

## 8. VATSIM-SPECIFIC RULES

### Connection Management
- Maintain stable connection
- Minimize disconnections
- Don't rapidly reconnect
- Report technical issues to dispatch

### Parking
- Use correct parking spot
- Don't block taxiways or runways
- Follow ramp procedures
- Request pushback before departure

### Flight Plan Filing
- File flight plan before departure
- Include cruise altitude
- Use correct callsign format
- File within 30 minutes of departure

**Initial Contact:**
```
"Boston Ground, AAX101, Boeing 737, with information Delta, 
request pushback, gate 42"
```

**Climb/Descent Requests:**
```
"Boston Departure, AAX101, requesting climb to Flight Level 280"
```

**Position Reports:**
```
"New York Center, AAX101, 50 north of BRINY, Flight Level 350,
```

- Speak clearly and at normal pace

## 13. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q: Can I file a flight plan after departure?**  
A: No. Always file before or immediately upon requesting pushback.

**Q: What if ATC assigns a runway I'm not comfortable with?**  
A: You can request a different runway, but follow ATC's decision.

**Q: Can I practice aerobatics?**  
A: No. Only in special restricted areas with ATC permission.

**Q: What do I do if I get disconnected?**  
A: Log back in and inform ATC of the issue if necessary.

**Q: How long can I park at a gate?**  
A: Follow realistic turnaround times (typically 30-60 minutes).
